1. The big picture

Read this picture from the outside in. A cluster is a group of brokers (servers). Brokers hold partitions. Partitions belong to topics and are made of records. Purple chips are the "leader" copy of a partition, teal chips are backup copies (module 4 explains them).

3. Topic: a named stream

A topic is a category of records with a name, such as orders, payments or user-clicks. Producers write to a topic; consumers subscribe to a topic. A topic is not one file: it is split into partitions.

AnalogyA topic is a TV channel. Producers broadcast on it. Viewers (consumers) tune in whenever they like, and each viewer has their own recording that they can rewind.

Naming tip: lowercase with dashes or dots, a clear business meaning (orders.placed). Do not mix dots and underscores in names, because Kafka metrics treat them alike.

4. Partition: where the data really lives

One topic is divided into partitions. A partition is an append-only, ordered log: new records are added at the end, and nothing is ever edited in the middle. Partitions exist for two reasons:

  • Scale: each partition can live on a different broker and be read by a different consumer, so work is shared.
  • Ordering: inside one partition, order is guaranteed. Across partitions, it is not.

How does a producer pick a partition? With a key: Kafka hashes the key and takes the remainder when dividing by the number of partitions. The same key always gives the same partition (as long as the partition count does not change). Try it below. This simulator uses Kafka's real hashing, and gives the same answers as your cluster.

5. Offset: the position in a partition

Each record in a partition gets a number called its offset: 0, 1, 2, 3 and so on. Offsets only go up, are never reused, and are unique only within one partition. So the full address of a record is:

topic + partition + offset  =  one exact record   (e.g. orders / 2 / 4)

Consumers remember "I have read up to offset N" for each partition. That bookmark is how they resume after a restart, and how they replay by moving it backwards. Module 5.

6. Ordering: the rule everyone trips over

ScopeIs order guaranteed?So what?
Inside one partitionYes, alwaysRecords come out in the order they went in.
Across partitions of a topicNoA consumer reading 3 partitions can interleave them in any order.
Same keyYes (same partition)Use the entity id as key: all events for one customer, device or order stay in sequence.
Design ruleDecide what must be ordered (usually "per customer" or "per order"), make that the key, and let everything else run in parallel across partitions.

7. Broker, cluster, controller

Broker

One Kafka server process. It stores partitions on disk and serves producers and consumers. Your lab has 3: kafka-0, kafka-1, kafka-2.

Cluster

A set of brokers working together. Clients connect to any broker, which tells them where each partition's leader is. That first address is the bootstrap server.

Controller

The brokers elect a few nodes (the KRaft quorum) that keep the cluster's "who is leader of what" records. In your lab all 3 brokers also act as controllers. Module 6.

8. Retention, in one paragraph

Because consumers do not delete records, Kafka needs another way to free disk. It removes old data by time (default 7 days) or size, whichever limit is hit first. Retention is not "after someone read it", it is "after it is old". You will tune and watch this in modules 6 and 9.
